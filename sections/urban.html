    Urban Growth Replicates, With Three Fixes

    Duranton and Puga (2023) estimate that relaxing planning limits in seven expensive US cities would raise national output by about 8%. An independent rewrite of their public code reproduces every headline number exactly, and that figure survives. Three coding errors change smaller numbers: freezing cities at their 1950 sizes costs about a fifth of consumption rather than a quarter, and Los Angeles enters the policy by only 22 dollars.

    Zipf's Law for US Cities, 2000-2024

    Zipf's law, under which a city's size is inversely proportional to its rank, is often treated as a constant of urban economics. In US data for 2000-2024 it holds only for metropolitan areas at particular cut-offs; incorporated cities follow a clean power law, but with an exponent clearly above 1.
