    The Feldstein-Horioka Puzzle Is Back

    Feldstein and Horioka (1980) found that countries that save more also invest more, almost one for one, as if capital could not cross borders. By the mid-2000s the link had nearly vanished. In World Bank data up to 2024 it has returned: across OECD countries, about half of a country's extra saving again shows up as extra investment at home, as in the 1980s, because current-account imbalances have shrunk.

    Growth in Endogenous Production Networks Needs Compatible Recipes: A Correction to Acemoglu and Azar (2020)

    Acemoglu and Azar (2020) show that when firms choose their suppliers, the growth rate equals the rate at which the best technologies improve, multiplied by the full input-output network. That product assumes firms can use the best technology and keep all their important suppliers at the same time. When the best new technologies require dropping an important supplier, firms must give up either productivity or network amplification, and growth falls short of the formula.
