# The Information Complexity of Nash Equilibrium

Equilibrium Daily, No. 0002, 8 November 2026. Section: Game Theory.

- Page: https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/notes/0002.html
- Full note with proof (PDF): https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/pdf/0002.pdf
- Drafted by an AI model and reviewed by a person before publication.

## Intuition

How many noisy observations of payoffs are needed to find an approximate Nash equilibrium of a small zero-sum game with high confidence? The answer depends only on the equilibrium mixing probabilities, is largest when equilibrium play is uniform, and can be far smaller than estimating every payoff first and then solving the game.

## Result

For a $3 \times 3$ zero-sum game $A$ with a unique, completely mixed equilibrium $(p,q)$, learned from noisy $N(A_{ij},1)$ queries, the expected number of observations needed to return an $\varepsilon$-equilibrium with probability $1-\delta$ satisfies $\lim_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \varepsilon^2 \lim_{\delta \downarrow 0} \frac{M_{\varepsilon,\delta}(A)}{\log(1/\delta)} = C(p,q) := 2\left[\min_{w \in \Delta_9^\circ} F_{p,q}(w)\right]^2$, with $0 < C(p,q) \le 8$ and $C(p,q) = 8$ if and only if $p = q = (1/3,1/3,1/3)$.

## Summary

Each query of payoff entry $(i,j)$ returns an independent $N(A_{ij},1)$ observation, and a procedure must return strategies with duality gap at most $\varepsilon$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ for every game in the class. The leading observation complexity, as confidence and then accuracy increase, is the constant $C(p,q)$, which depends on the game only through its equilibrium probabilities; $F_{p,q}(w)$ is a transport-type functional over couplings of $p$ and $q$ given a sampling allocation $w$ over the nine entries. The constant is maximal, equal to 8, exactly at uniform equilibrium, and for the family $A_r$ with equilibrium $(1-2r, r, r)$ it is of order $r^2$. By comparison, estimating all nine payoffs to maximum-norm error $\varepsilon$ has coefficient 18, and estimating payoffs to error $\varepsilon/2$ and then solving the game has coefficient 72. The proof applies the infinite-answer pure-exploration characterization of Poiani, Bernasconi and Celli (2026) and evaluates its small-accuracy limit through transportation duality.
