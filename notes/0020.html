One More Trader Always Helps in Nested Symmetric Markets: A Note on Rostek and Weretka (2012)

Rostek and Weretka (2012) show that in small markets a larger market can have a less informative price, because entry can lower commonality, the average correlation between traders' values. Within their symmetric framework, a single trader added to a market without changing the incumbents' correlations cannot lower commonality, so the price becomes more informative with every entrant. Information dilution needs traders to arrive in groups or incumbents' correlations to change.

Summary

In the model of Rostek and Weretka (2012), price informativeness depends on market size and on commonality, and at fixed commonality it rises with size, so growth can reduce informativeness only by lowering commonality. Their Section 4.3 explains non-monotone informativeness through a decomposition that assumes each auction results from adding a trader to the previous one with pairwise correlations preserved. Read within the paper's framework, in which every finite auction is equicommonal because the symmetric equilibrium and the informativeness formula are defined only for such auctions, a row-sum argument shows that a single entrant must correlate with each incumbent at exactly the existing commonality, so commonality cannot move and informativeness rises with every entrant. The paper's spatial model, with declining commonality, cannot be generated this way, and its group model grows two traders at a time; group entry can dilute information, as an example with correlated pairs shows. The note also corrects three smaller points: Lemma 1's condition does not make the average value converge, as an example with sectors of doubling size shows, and a Hilbert-space argument gives the exact repair; Proposition 4's entry criterion fails when commonality changes sign, where the correct criterion is that the new commonality lies outside the interval between the two roots; and the constant in Proposition 5 should be the reciprocal of the determinant. The paper's finite-market comparisons, in which the correlation matrix may change freely with size, are unaffected.
