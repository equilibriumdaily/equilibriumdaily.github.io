# The Pre-FOMC Drift Lost Its Morning

Equilibrium Daily, No. 0003, 8 November 2026. Section: Asset Pricing.

- Page: https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/notes/0003.html
- Full note with proof (PDF): https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/pdf/0003.pdf
- Drafted by an AI model and reviewed by a person before publication.

## Intuition

Stocks have earned a well-known excess return in the hours before Federal Reserve announcements. Studies using daily closing prices conclude that this drift has faded, but it has moved rather than disappeared: since 2011 it is earned overnight before the announcement day, not during the morning of that day.

## Result

The pre-FOMC announcement drift in SPY has not disappeared; its timing has changed. From September 2003 to March 2011 most of it accrued between the open and 2pm on the announcement day (29.1 bp, $t = 4.3$); after March 2011 that morning component is zero (1.5 bp), and the overnight return into the announcement day (17.2 bp, $t = 3.7$) is now essentially the whole drift.

## Summary

Lucca and Moench (2015) documented a large excess stock return in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC announcements, and studies using daily closing prices report that it weakened after 2015. Using SPY from September 2003 to December 2024 (170 scheduled announcements), with CRSP open and close prices and TAQ quote mid-points at 2pm, the note splits the window from 2pm on the previous day to the close of the following day into seven legs and compares each leg on announcement days with all other days. The 2pm-to-2pm drift falls from 58.0 bp to 16.9 bp, and the fall of 27.6 bp in the morning leg has $t = -3.6$, while the overnight leg is stable at about 13 to 17 bp and holds separately in 2011-2015 and 2016-2024. Closing-price studies understate the remaining drift because returns after the announcement have been weak since 2011. The overnight result survives alternative price sources, the exclusion of 2020, winsorization, placebo nights and the removal of the 2011-2012 meetings with a 12:30pm statement.
