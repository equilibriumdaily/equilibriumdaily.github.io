Who Bargains Next? Recognition and Cooperative Allocations

Who proposes after an offer is rejected looks like a procedural detail, but it changes the outcome of coalitional bargaining. If the player who rejects proposes next, patient players no longer reach the Shapley value: every player's deviation from an equal share is amplified.

Summary

Hart and Mas-Colell (1996) showed that random proposers with a risk that a rejected proposer drops out lead patient players to the Shapley value. This note compares two anonymous protocols for three players with transferable utility that share coalition opportunities, discounting and departure risk, and differ only in who proposes after a surviving rejection: a uniformly drawn player (P0P_0P0​) or the rejector (P1P_1P1​). Every subgame-perfect equilibrium, including mixed and history-dependent ones, agrees immediately on the grand coalition, and each protocol has a unique payoff vector.
