Growth in Endogenous Production Networks Needs Compatible Recipes: A Correction to Acemoglu and Azar (2020)

Acemoglu and Azar (2020) show that when firms choose their suppliers, the growth rate equals the rate at which the best technologies improve, multiplied by the full input-output network. That product assumes firms can use the best technology and keep all their important suppliers at the same time. When the best new technologies require dropping an important supplier, firms must give up either productivity or network amplification, and growth falls short of the formula.

Summary

In the endogenous production network model of Acemoglu and Azar (2020), industries choose supplier sets from all available input combinations, and Theorem 6 states that growth equals the frontier productivity rate DDD times the Leontief multiplier of the full potential network. The note gives two economies that satisfy every stated assumption but contradict the formula. In the first, recipes that use the supplier carrying half of all input shares are unproductive, so every firm drops it and growth is 4/34/34/3 rather than 222; the same holds with independent continuous productivity draws, where growth can take any value in [4/3,2)[4/3, 2)[4/3,2). In the second, late-arriving industries with very high productivity levels raise growth to 444, because the assumption on the frontier holds industry by industry but not uniformly. The failures trace to two specific steps in the proof and overturn Corollaries 1 to 3, the upper bound of Theorem 7 and Theorem B3, while sustained positive growth, the benchmark with iid Gumbel or exponential draws and the static model survive; the corrected theorem holds exactly when frontier technologies are compatible with the existing supplier base.
