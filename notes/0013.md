# Urban Growth Replicates, With Three Fixes

Equilibrium Daily, No. 0013, 11 November 2026. Section: Urban Economics.

- Page: https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/notes/0013.html
- Full note with proof (PDF): https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/pdf/0013.pdf
- Drafted by an AI model and reviewed by a person before publication.

## Intuition

Duranton and Puga (2023) estimate that relaxing planning limits in seven expensive US cities would raise national output by about 8%. An independent rewrite of their public code reproduces every headline number exactly, and that figure survives. Three coding errors change smaller numbers: freezing cities at their 1950 sizes costs about a fifth of consumption rather than a quarter, and Los Angeles enters the policy by only 22 dollars.

## Result

Re-coded from the authors' public files, the headline results of Duranton and Puga (2023) reproduce to six decimals, including the 7.95% output gain when seven cities expand. Three coding errors are confirmed: the consumption loss from freezing cities at their 1950 sizes is 19.4%, not 26.8%; the seven-city consumption gain is 2.11%, not 2.16%; and Los Angeles clears the policy's 200,000 dollar price cutoff by 22 dollars under a conversion that depends on the omitted city, although every standard conversion keeps it in. City edges spread with population at elasticity 0.41 rather than the model's 1, but urban costs measured directly rise at elasticity 0.104, close to the model's 0.11.

## Summary

Duranton and Puga (2023) model incumbents who use planning rules to cap city size and find that relaxing permits in seven expensive US cities would raise output by 7.95%. Porting the authors' Stata programs to Python reproduces their regressions and every headline number; only regressions on restricted NLSY79 and NHTS locations could not be re-run. Three errors are confirmed. Log prices are converted to dollars with the squared standard error of each city dummy, which depends on the omitted city: Los Angeles clears the cutoff by 22 dollars, misses it by 302 dollars (output gain 5.68%) with itself omitted or with no correction, and lies between 226,000 and 229,000 dollars under residual variance, Duan smearing or full prediction variance, which keep the same seven cities. The frozen-city calculation credits newcomers with incumbent consumption, contrary to the paper's own accounting, so the loss is 19.4% rather than 26.8%; the original figure holds only if permit payments are rebated, in which case the seven-city consumption gain would be 4.15%. Stata's min() ignoring missing values drops 774,360 residents of ten emptied cities, and counting them moves that gain from 2.16% to 2.11%. Equation (29)'s exact Pareto distribution of city sizes requires continuous time, although approximate Zipf survives. The linear city implies edge distance proportional to population, while the data give elasticity 0.41 (s.e. 0.02); center house prices plus marginal consumption rise at 0.104 (s.e. 0.010), close to the model's cost elasticity of 0.11, so the mechanism holds in reduced form.
