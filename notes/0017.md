# Noise Beats Coarseness: The Value of Inside Information in the Kyle-Back Model

Equilibrium Daily, No. 0017, 12 November 2026. Section: Market Microstructure.

- Page: https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/notes/0017.html
- Full note with proof (PDF): https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/pdf/0017.pdf
- Drafted by an AI model and reviewed by a person before publication.

## Intuition

Two signals that explain the same share of a normal fundamental's variance can be worth very different amounts to an insider in the continuous-time Kyle model. The sign of the fundamental explains 63.7 percent of its variance and earns 63.7 percent of the full-information profit, while normal noisy information of the same precision earns 79.8 percent. The insider must steer order flow to a normally distributed endpoint to stay hidden, so information whose shape is far from normal is worth less.

## Result

In the Kyle-Back model with a normal fundamental, for any signal the insider's profit as a share of the full-information profit lies between $R^2$ and $R$, where $R^2$ is the share of fundamental variance the signal explains. The upper bound holds exactly when the insider's valuation is normal, and the lower bound exactly when it is a nondecreasing function of the fundamental, as for information that sorts the fundamental into ordered categories. Among signals with at most $n$ messages the best is the Lloyd-Max quantizer cell of the normal law, earning $1 - D_n$: 0.637, 0.810 and 0.883 for $n = 2, 3, 4$.

## Summary

In Back's (1992) equilibrium the insider's expected profit equals $\sigma_u$ times the largest covariance between his valuation and a standard normal variable, because total order flow must end at a normal point for the insider to stay hidden. Cauchy-Schwarz gives the upper bound $R$, attained only by normal valuations, and coupling the valuation with the fundamental itself gives the lower bound $R^2$, attained exactly by monotone coarse information such as the sign of the fundamental, thresholds or interval categories. In general the profit share is $R\rho$, where $\rho$ is the largest correlation between the valuation and a normal variable: $2/\pi$ in square root for two-point laws, 0.977 for uniform and 0.910 for Student $t_3$. With $n$ messages the best signal reports the Lloyd-Max cell, with thresholds at $\pm 0.612\sigma_v$ for three categories and at 0 and $\pm 0.982\sigma_v$ for four, and noise or randomization cannot help; by Shannon's bound one bit of information is worth at most $\sqrt{3}/2$ of perfect foresight. Two consequences follow inside the model. The covariation of price changes with order flow equals the value of information for every signal; the ratio of fundamental variance to price impact divides it by $(R\rho)^2$, overstating the value of sign information by $(\pi/2)^2 = 2.47$, and the ratio of price variance to price impact divides it by $\rho^2$, still overstating it by $\pi/2$. Non-normal information also reaches prices more slowly: by date $t$ prices reflect at most the share $t$ of the insider's information, and for sign information the share is $(2/\pi)\arcsin t$, one third at $t = 1/2$.
