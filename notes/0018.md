# Publish the Rest: Research Funded by Trading Rents in a Kyle-Back Market

Equilibrium Daily, No. 0018, 12 November 2026. Section: Information Economics.

- Page: https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/notes/0018.html
- Full note with proof (PDF): https://equilibriumdaily.github.io/pdf/0018.pdf
- Drafted by an AI model and reviewed by a person before publication.

## Intuition

Much research is paid for by the trading profits it makes possible, but keeping it private drives liquidity traders away. In a Kyle-Back market with endogenous participation, if all output must stay private the best signal is coarse, and every Gaussian private signal can be worse than no research. If part of the output can be published, the optimum keeps private only a small Gaussian component whose trading rent exactly covers the research bill and publishes the rest.

## Result

In a Kyle-Back market where liquidity traders with uniformly distributed benefits choose whether to participate, research costing $F$ with information budget $k$ nats can be funded through trading rents if and only if $F \le B/4$ and $d_F \le R_k$, where $n_F = (1 + \sqrt{1 - 4F/B})/2$, $d_F = F^2/(\kappa^2 n_F)$ and $R_k = 1 - e^{-2k}$. Over all joint public and private experiments, maximal welfare is $B(n_F - n_F^2/2) + \eta R_k - F$, attained by keeping private only an independent normal component of variance $d_F$ and publishing a normal component of variance $R_k - d_F$; this split also maximizes price informativeness at every trading date. If all output must stay private, a threshold signal can be optimal over all experiments.

## Summary

The insider's profit is a loss to liquidity traders, who participate only if their benefits exceed it, so the high participation equilibrium solves $\kappa a = B\sqrt{n}(1 - n)$, where $a$ is the insider's profit coefficient from the Kyle-Back pricing of any signal. Because $a$ lies between the explained variance $r$ and $\sqrt{r}$, with ordered categories at the bottom and normal signals at the top, a coarse signal can sustain a market that equally accurate normal information would shut down. When all research output must stay private, welfare is bounded by a strictly concave function of participation and a threshold signal attains the bound: with $B = \kappa = \eta = 1$, $F = 0.1$ and a one-bit budget the optimum is the signal $1\{V > 1.312\}$, with welfare 0.633 against 0.5 without research, while every Gaussian private signal does worse than no research. When the producer can publish, the private component only needs to earn the rent that pays for the research, and normal information earns the most rent per unit of private variance, so the global optimum over all joint experiments keeps private a Gaussian component of variance $d_F$ and publishes the rest; in the example the private part explains 0.011 of fundamental variance, the public part 0.739, and welfare rises to 1.144. With research costing $F + ck$, the optimal amount solves a strictly concave problem in which each unit of cost is scaled up by $n/(2n - 1)$, the liquidity lost to finance one more unit of rent.
