# Who Bargains Next? Recognition and Cooperative Allocations

## Intuition

Who proposes after an offer is rejected looks like a procedural detail, but it changes the outcome of coalitional bargaining. If the player who rejects proposes next, patient players no longer reach the Shapley value: every player's deviation from an equal share is amplified.

## Result

In three-player coalitional bargaining with proposer departure probability $q$, both protocols have a unique subgame-perfect equilibrium payoff vector with immediate efficient agreement: $E_0 = e + d(s-e)$ when the next proposer after a surviving rejection is drawn uniformly, and $E_1 = e + d(1+a)(s-e)$ when the rejector proposes next, where $s$ is the Shapley value, $e$ the equal split, $a = \delta(1-q)$ and $d = \delta q/(1-a)$. The patient limits are $s$ and $s + (1-q)(s-e)$, which coincide if and only if $c_1 = c_2 = c_3$.

## Summary

Hart and Mas-Colell (1996) showed that random proposers with a risk that a rejected proposer drops out lead patient players to the Shapley value. This note compares two anonymous protocols for three players with transferable utility that share coalition opportunities, discounting and departure risk, and differ only in who proposes after a surviving rejection: a uniformly drawn player ($P_0$) or the rejector ($P_1$). Every subgame-perfect equilibrium, including mixed and history-dependent ones, agrees immediately on the grand coalition, and each protocol has a unique payoff vector. Under $P_1$ every player's patient deviation from the equal share is multiplied by $2-q$, so players whose Shapley value exceeds one third gain and the others lose; for $q = 7/8$ and $(c_1, c_2, c_3) = (1/4, 1/20, 1/20)$ the patient payoffs are $(4/15, 11/30, 11/30)$ under $P_0$ and $(31/120, 89/240, 89/240)$ under $P_1$. Specifying who controls bargaining after disagreement is therefore part of specifying the outcome.
